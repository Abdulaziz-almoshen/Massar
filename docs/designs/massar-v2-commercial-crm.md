## Problem Statement

Lean's product and sales departments run one commercial engine and cannot see each other. A product
manager finishes market research, picks target accounts, and hands them to sales as a spreadsheet or
a pasted list. Another department does the same for its own product. Sales adds leads from their own
connections. From that moment the product side is blind: nothing records that the handoff happened,
so nobody can ask what became of it.

In the founder's words: *"as a product, I can see visibly in front of me where the customers that I
targeted, what happened to them in the sales"* and *"so we can learn from the feedback from the
sales."* The system must also know *"what product this customer is onboarding in this CRM for, when
was it onboarding"*, act on it by call, visit, WhatsApp or campaign, move it through stages, let a
rep pick a product and reach every account onboarded for it, and let the AI read what happened and
suggest what to do next.

## Root cause

Two records are missing. Everything above is a query over them.

1. **The handoff.** A named person in a named department gives sales a named list for a named
   product on a date, and that creates an obligation someone can audit later. Today it is a string
   appended to a JSON array with no who, no when and no department.
2. **The engagement.** An account, a channel, an actor, a time, and what happened. Today WhatsApp
   lives in `events` and `messages`, and calls and visits live nowhere. `opportunities.source`
   records where a deal came from once; it is not an interaction log.

A screen redesign without these two tables produces the same blindness in a nicer font.

## Landscape

Salesforce models the closest thing with Campaign Member: a link between a lead and a campaign
carrying a status. That records **exposure**. A handoff records an **obligation**, has two parties,
and has no standard CRM object. Codex, reading the requirements cold without this conversation,
independently described the product as "every customer handoff becomes an owned, auditable
product-specific obligation."

CRM adoption research is uniform: reps spend roughly a quarter of their time selling, logging fails
because the logged data serves managers and returns nothing to the person entering it, and mandates
produce a compliance spike then a relapse.

**EUREKA.** Everyone concludes "remove manual logging" because in every other CRM the data only
flows upward. Massar can invert it. The AI already reads a conversation and produces a next action.
If tapping a call outcome returns a drafted Arabic follow-up and a re-ranked queue within seconds,
logging becomes the fastest route to the next action rather than a tax. This is a binding design
constraint and a **kill criterion** (see phase 6), not a feature.

## Premises (all agreed)

1. The gap is two missing records, not the screens.
2. The core object is a handoff, not a tag: sender, receiver, product, date, auditable obligation.
3. Every logging action must pay the rep back within seconds, or the form dies in week two.
4. WhatsApp stops being the schema's centre. **Nine tables carry a phone column** (`contacts`,
   `messages`, `interest_tags`, `events`, `entities`, `contact_insights`, `campaign_targets`,
   `opportunities`, `opp_auto`) and two more (`tasks`, `notes`) link by phone through `ref_id`.
   `contacts.phone` is a primary key, so a clinic with no WhatsApp number, or two people at one
   clinic, cannot be modelled. The account becomes the spine; WhatsApp becomes one channel.
5. Doing nothing keeps the product side blind, because nothing records that the handoff happened.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Chosen |
|---|---|---|
| D2 | Identity | Real accounts with roles and departments. No SSO, no invitations. |
| D3 | Data grain | One account, **many product tracks**, each with its own stage, owner and history. |
| D4/D7 | Stages | Per-product stage sets as data, every stage mapped to a standard bucket. The visual builder with branching and required fields is **gated on the 90-day test** (owner: the founder; clock starts the day the second product's stage set goes live: ~week 9 unbuffered, ~week 10 as planned). |
| D5 | Engagement capture | One tap, plus optional Arabic voice note, plus a structured outcome form. The tap alone is a complete record. |
| D8 | Account identity | Full: accounts and contacts, duplicate detection, reversible merge with history, parent and branch hierarchy. |
| D9 | Build path | **Massar v2 as a new application**, parallel run against the same database, per-surface cutover. |

**Recorded reversals.** D5 partially reverses the "zero typing for reps" rule from this morning's
design doc; reconciled by making the tap alone complete. D7 revises D4 after Codex named the full
pipeline builder as most likely to be regretted and supplied a falsifiable test.

**Ownership, resolved.** An account has **no owner**. Ownership is per track
(`product_tracks.owner_user_id`), so two departments own their own tracks on one clinic. This closes
what was an open question in the first draft and unblocks phase 5.

## Cross-model perspective

Codex agreed on the handoff as the core object and on the build sequence. It contributed the hole
this analysis missed: a hospital is not a phone number, and without canonical accounts, contact
relationships, deduplication and merge history every downstream dashboard is unreliable. That
produced D8. It named fully configurable pipelines as most likely to be regretted, with a test at 90
days: count pipelines actually modified, whether stage mappings needed hand reconciliation, and
whether managers exported to Excel. That produced D7.

**Where this plan disagrees with Codex.** It advised against building the AI layer until clean
engagement data exists. It could not see how mature Massar's already is: conversation reading, the
earned seriousness signal and the suggested next action are shipped and live. Extending that to call
and visit notes is incremental. It stays.

## Data model

### Identity and authorization
```
users              (id, name, email, password_hash, department_id, role, active, created_at)
                    role ∈ admin | product_manager | sales_manager | sales_rep
departments        (id, name, kind ∈ product|sales|other, created_at)
sessions           (token, user_id, issued_at, expires_at, revoked_at)
```

**Permission matrix.** Full identity is worth nothing without this; it was the one unpriced
consequence of D8 in the first draft.

| Object | admin | product_manager | sales_manager | sales_rep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| accounts, contacts | RW | R all | RW all | **C** new, RW own-created or where owns a track |
| dupe_candidates | RW | RW raised by own import | RW all | R own |
| merge / unmerge | RW | — | RW | — |
| handoffs | RW | RW own department | R all | R where recipient |
| handoff_items | RW | R own department's | RW all | RW own |
| product_tracks | RW | R where product is their department's | RW all | **C** with self as owner, RW own |
| engagements | RW | **R** all (this is R6/R7) | RW all | RW own |
| pipelines, stages | RW | RW own department's products | R | R |
| users, departments | RW | — | R | — |
| campaigns / send | RW | R | RW | RW own product |

A product manager reads everything about their own product's tracks and writes nothing on them. That
asymmetry is the point: the handoff creates an obligation they can audit, not a pipeline they run.

Two rows in that table were wrong in the second draft and are corrected here. A **rep can create an
account**, or R3 ("sales bring their own leads") has no path, since a brand-new lead has no track to
own. And a **product manager resolves duplicates raised by their own import**, or phase 4's proof
deadlocks: a PM importing fifty accounts would stop at the first collision and wait for an admin.
Merging two established accounts stays with a sales manager or an admin, because that moves money
between records.

### Catalogue and process
```
products           (id, name, department_id, kb_ref, active, created_at)
pipelines          (id, product_id NULL⇒default, name, created_at)
pipeline_stages    (id, pipeline_id, key, label_ar, position, is_terminal,
                    standard_bucket ∈ contact|present|evaluate|negotiate|won|lost)
form_schemas       (id, product_id NULL⇒default, channel, version, fields JSONB, active, created_at)
```
`standard_bucket` keeps cross-department funnels comparable while stages differ; it is what makes D7
safe. **Bucket map from today's enum:** `contact→contact`, `present→present`, **`tech→evaluate`**,
`negotiate→negotiate`, `won→won`, `lost→lost`. The `tech`→`evaluate` rename is deliberate and must
be applied by the migration; the first draft renamed it silently.

`form_schemas` is what D5's "per-product outcome fields without a migration" actually rests on.
`engagements.form_schema_id` points at the exact schema row a capture was made against — a bare
version integer would not identify one, since schemas are scoped by product and channel — so editing
a schema never silently re-interprets historical rows.

### Accounts (the spine)
```
accounts           (id, name, name_normalized, city, size, sector, parent_account_id NULL,
                    attrs JSONB, facts JSONB, created_by, created_at, merged_into_id NULL)
account_contacts   (id, account_id, name, role_title, phone_e164, email,
                    is_primary, created_at, merged_away BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE)
                    UNIQUE (account_id, phone_e164) WHERE phone_e164 IS NOT NULL
phone_routing      (phone_e164 PRIMARY KEY, account_contact_id, updated_at)
account_merges     (id, source_account_id, target_account_id, merged_by, merged_at,
                    repointed JSONB)   -- explicit [(table, row_id, old_account_id)] tuples
dupe_candidates    (id, a_account_id, b_account_id, score, reason,
                    status ∈ open|merged|distinct, resolved_by, resolved_at)
```

**Routing is unique; reality is not.** An inbound Gupshup webhook must resolve to exactly one
contact, but a global unique index on the phone would have forbidden one doctor being a contact at
two clinics, and it would have blocked its own merge reversal: unmerging restores a contact whose
number the target already holds, so reversal would fail precisely where merges are most likely to be
wrong. The split fixes both. `account_contacts` is unique **per account**, so the same doctor may
appear at two. `phone_routing` names the single contact an inbound message routes to, is rewritten
on merge and on unmerge, and is the only thing the webhook reads. **When resolution fails** (an
unknown number replies), v2 creates an account named for the number, one contact, one routing row
and no track, then raises a dupe candidate against any name match — which is what `ensureEntity`
does today, stated rather than implied.

**Opt-out has one writer and one truth.** `account_contacts` carries **no** `opted_out` column. The
WhatsApp runtime table stays the sole writer, and v2 reads opt-out through a view over it. Two
stores for the one flag that must never regress is exactly this codebase's recurring defect class,
applied to the sacred boolean.

**Name normalization** (`name_normalized`), needed before any dedup score means anything, and not a
thing the codebase does today — `audience.ts` folds alef for header matching only:
fold `أإآ→ا`, `ة→ه`, `ى→ي`, strip tatweel `ـ` and diacritics, collapse whitespace, drop a leading
`ال`, and drop the leading entity word (`مجمع`, `مركز`, `عيادات`, `مستشفى`, `صيدلية`) into a
separate token rather than deleting it.
**Thresholds:** score ≥ 0.85 on (normalized name, city) or an exact phone match blocks the import
row and opens a dupe candidate. **There is no auto-merge.** Every merge is a human decision, because
an account carries money.

**Merge reversal, defined.** `repointed` holds the explicit list of `(table, row_id,
old_account_id)` tuples the merge moved. Reversal restores exactly those. Rows written *after* the
merge stay on the target: reversal is an undo, not a time machine, and the doc says so rather than
leaving it to be discovered.

### The handoff
```
handoffs           (id, from_user_id, from_department_id,
                    to_department_id, to_user_id NULL,          -- the second party
                    product_id, title, note,
                    method ∈ import|manual|api, sla_days DEFAULT 14, created_at)
handoff_items      (id, handoff_id, account_id, track_id NULL, assigned_user_id NULL,
                    status ∈ pending|accepted|rejected|duplicate, resolved_at)
```
`handoff_items` is the obligation ledger. R6 and R7 are one query against it. A handoff names both
parties, because premise 2 says the object has two and the second draft recorded only the sender;
`to_user_id` is null when a list goes to a department rather than a named rep, and `assigned_user_id`
carries the per-account assignment a sales manager makes afterwards. `sla_days` defaults to **14**
so "overdue" is computable on day one; confirming that number is an open question, not a blocker.

### Tracks
```
product_tracks     (id, account_id, product_id, pipeline_id, stage_id, owner_user_id,
                    source ∈ handoff|rep_own|inbound|referral|campaign,
                    source_handoff_id NULL, sale_price, years, qty, discount,
                    opened_at, stage_at, closed_at, close_reason)
track_stage_history(id, track_id, from_stage_id, to_stage_id, moved_by, moved_at, note)
UNIQUE (account_id, product_id) WHERE closed_at IS NULL
```
The partial unique index stops two departments opening duplicate live tracks on one account for one
product. **Existing `opportunities` rows can violate it** — nothing enforces one line per
(account, product) today — so the migration dedupes duplicate live product lines before the index is
created, keeping the oldest and closing the rest with `close_reason = 'merged at migration'`.

```
track_claims       (account_id, product_id, first_claimed_at, claimed_by, track_id NULL,
                    PRIMARY KEY (account_id, product_id))
```
This is `opp_auto` carried forward, re-keyed. Its rule is unchanged and must survive the rebuild
verbatim: **a claim is once-only, and a track a human deleted is never re-created by the agent.**
Deleting an auto-opened track is a decision, and a system that undoes it is worse than one that
never opened it. Omitting this table was the most dangerous gap in the first draft.

**The claim grain widens, and that is a decision, not a detail.** v1 keys the claim on
`(phone, product-name)`; v2 keys it on `(account_id, product_id)`. After D8's merges, several phones
collapse into one account, so the migration is many-to-one and the rule cannot survive "verbatim"
however much one would like it to. **Earliest `first_claimed_at` wins**, the losing claims are kept
in the merge history rather than dropped, and the consequence is stated plainly: a claim burned
through one contact now blocks that product for the whole account. That is the correct reading of
the original rule at the new grain — the rule was always about one deal per account per product, and
the phone was only ever standing in for the account.

**Only one claim ledger may be live at a time.** v1 writes `opp_auto` on every hot reading
(`tracker.ts` → `db.autoOppFromHot`, plus a boot backfill). If v2 also owned claims during the
parallel run, a human deleting a v2 track would write v2's ledger while v1's agent, reading v1's,
opened the opportunity again and the cutover backfill resurrected it — the precise failure this rule
exists to prevent, reintroduced by the parallel run itself. **The handover is a single switch on a
config flag that already exists:** `cfg.autoOppFromHot` stays ON in v1 until phase 5 ships, and is
turned OFF the same day v2 assumes claims. Before that day v2 opens no tracks automatically; after
it, v1 opens no opportunities. The switch is a phase-5 exit condition.

### Engagements
```
engagements        (id, account_id, track_id NULL, contact_id NULL, actor_user_id,
                    channel ∈ call|visit|whatsapp|campaign|email|other,
                    direction ∈ out|in, occurred_at, outcome_id,
                    note_text NULL, form JSONB, form_schema_id NULL,
                    voice_asset_id NULL, source ∈ manual|agent|webhook, created_at)
engagement_outcomes(id, product_id NULL⇒default, channel NULL⇒all,
                    key, label_ar, is_positive, sort)
voice_assets       (id, storage_key, mime, duration_ms, bytes_len,
                    transcript NULL, transcribed_at NULL, delete_after)
reminders          (id, track_id NULL, account_id, due_at, assigned_to, reason,
                    origin ∈ rep|ai, created_by, done_at NULL)
```
`track_id` is nullable because a first call can precede any track.

**Voice audio does not live in Postgres.** `product_assets` stores one blob per product; this would
be every rep, every call, every day, in the database the parallel run already reads. Audio goes to
object storage keyed by `storage_key`, capped at 120 seconds and 2 MB, and `delete_after` retains it
30 days. The audio is deleted; the transcript is retained with its track and deleted when the
account is.

**Consent, not just residency.** The second draft covered where Arabic transcription runs and said
nothing about the right to record a customer's voice at all. Under Saudi PDPL, in health-tech, that
is the load-bearing half. Two rules: the rep's note is **the rep speaking, never the customer's
recorded audio** — a dictated summary after the call, not a recording of it — and the capture screen
says so in Arabic where the rep taps. Recording a customer requires a separate legal answer and is
out of scope here.

### Carried forward, explicitly
| Today | v2 |
|---|---|
| `opp_auto` | `track_claims`, re-keyed to `(account_id, product_id)`, rule intact |
| `tags` (the closed product registry) | becomes `products`; every tag name migrates as a product owned by a department |
| `notes` (`ref_kind='contact'`) | migrate to `engagements` with `channel='other'`, `source='manual'`, author preserved as `actor_user_id` |
| `tasks` (`ref_kind='contact'`) | migrate to `reminders` on the account, `assigned_to` preserved |
| `product_kb`, `product_assets` | unchanged, re-pointed at `products.id` |
| `campaigns`, `campaign_targets` | re-pointed at accounts and contacts, **with the guard below** |
| `contact_insights` | **stays keyed to the conversation** (`contact_id`), not the track |
| `messages`, `contacts`, `events` | stay as the WhatsApp runtime's own tables |

**Insights stay conversation-keyed.** `getInsights` computes from one phone's transcript and caches
by phone. A conversation with no track has no track key, and an account with two tracks has one
WhatsApp thread, so the mapping is 1:1 in neither direction. Per-track views are derived on read.
The same rule settles what the seriousness meter shows on an account with two contacts: **the score
belongs to a conversation, and the account record shows the most recently active one, labelled with
whose it is.**

**The duplicate-send guard stays a database constraint, not an application check.**
`campaign_targets` is `PRIMARY KEY (campaign_id, phone)` today, which makes a double send to one
number structurally impossible. Re-keying to a contact would let two contact rows holding one number
both receive a blast, and that touches the number's quality rating. v2 **adds `account_contact_id`
alongside the existing `phone` column and keeps the existing `PRIMARY KEY (campaign_id, phone)`
untouched**. The column is not renamed: v1's campaign reads still select `phone`, and they run
read-only until phase 7. The guarantee is therefore additive and structural, not migrated. Saying "a guard on
the send path" would have quietly demoted a structural guarantee to a code path anyone can bypass,
which is this codebase's own defect class.

A department's user hands accounts to sales for a product; each handoff item becomes a product
track; every call, visit and WhatsApp exchange on that track is an engagement; the product manager
reads the handoff back through its items and their engagements.

## Approaches considered

**A. Extend in place.** New tables in the existing engine, new screens as modules inside
`dashboard.ts`. Effort L, risk medium. Rejected: eight new screens inside a 4,100-line template
literal governed by an anchored-edits-only rule, after that file already shipped a blank page once.

**B. New core plus a real front end.** Identity, accounts, handoffs, tracks and engagements added to
the existing Fastify app behind a JSON API; new screens in a proper front end; WhatsApp becomes a
channel adapter. Effort XL, risk medium.

**C. Massar v2, new application, parallel run, per-surface cutover.** Effort XXL, risk high.

## Chosen approach: C

**This review recommended B. The founder chose C**, knowing the price. That over-ride is recorded
rather than blurred. Three mitigations turn its risks into managed ones.

1. **Safety-critical code is ported, not rewritten.** `gupshup.ts` (the only file that knows the
   wire format), opt-out enforcement, agent turn caps and window checks, the pure business tier
   (`signal-domain.ts`, `opps-domain.ts`, `facts.ts`, `audience.ts`) and all **15 check scripts (16
   steps in `npm run check`, counting the test run)** move across as source. Rewriting them would
   mean re-earning production behaviour that already holds.
2. **v2 reads the same database; it does not copy it.** During the parallel run v2 is read-only on
   the carried tables and reads them through views. There is no "one-time migration" running ahead
   of a 22-week parallel period, because every row v1 wrote after such a copy would be invisible to
   v2 — `autoOppFromHot` alone would silently create opportunity rows for the whole period. One
   backfill runs at cutover, and it is idempotent and re-runnable.

   **Four named write windows, and no others.** "Read-only" was too absolute in the second draft
   and the phases contradicted it. (a) **Phase 5 start:** the one-off close of duplicate live
   `opportunities` lines, so the partial unique index can be created. (b) **Phase 5, the same day:** v1 stops
   writing `opportunities` and `opp_auto` altogether, because v2 takes ownership of tracks — see
   below. This one closes a carried table rather than opening it. (c) **Phase 7:** v2 becomes the writer for `campaigns`
   and `campaign_targets`, and v1's campaign screens go read-only the same day. (d) **Phase 9:** the
   `tech→evaluate` bucket rename, applied with the backfill. Every other carried table stays read to
   v2 until cutover.

   **Phase 5 is a per-surface cutover, not a flag flip.** `product_tracks` is a real table, not a
   view: a view cannot carry `stage_id`, `owner_user_id` or `track_stage_history`. So from the day
   it takes over, v2 is the only writer of pipeline state and **v1's «فرص البيع» goes read-only the
   same day** — its manual create and bulk «افتح فرصة» are disabled together with
   `cfg.autoOppFromHot`. Otherwise humans would write v1's board and v2's tracks for the
   remaining eleven weeks of the programme, a second dual-write hiding behind the first one this
   plan just closed. One surface, one writer, one
   day.
3. **Every phase is proven on a seeded copy of production with a named human witness.** A new
   application with no users has nothing to feature-flag, so "behind a flag" would have been a
   comforting fiction. The proof column below is the real gate.

**24.5 weeks of work, not the ~20 first quoted and not the 22.5 of the second draft.** The column
sums to 2+2+3+2+2.5+2+3+2.5+2.5+3. With a 15% buffer for parallel-run overhead, plan on
**28 weeks**. Two drafts of this document got that sum wrong; it is now computed, not estimated.
Phases 1 to 8 are 19.5 weeks unbuffered, so the parallel period runs roughly **22 weeks**.

**Kill criterion (phase 6).** The queue and the tap ship in week 1 of the phase so the three-week
window closes before phase 7 begins; in the second draft the verdict would have landed after phase 7
was already built, which is a gate that cannot gate. Measured per rep-day, never per track: a rep holding thirty open
tracks cannot tap thirty times a day, and the second draft's wording would have failed a healthy
system by arithmetic. If, three weeks after the rep queue reaches one real rep, that rep records
**fewer than three engagements on a median working day**, or fewer than **half the calls they say
they made** appear within 24 hours, phases 7 to 9 do not ship as designed. The programme stops and
returns to the tap-only record. A 28-week programme needs a stop rule more than a short one does.

## The Assignment

## Engineering review (2026-09-03) — 13 findings, all decided

Reviewed with `/plan-eng-review` after approval. Every decision below is the founder's.

### Scope (Step 0)
- **A thin handoff pilot ships in week 3**, against today's `entities` table: two tables plus a
  submitter column, deliberately throwaway, deleted when phase 4 lands. It exists because the
  approved plan is dark for seven weeks and the founder's own question («what happened to the
  customers I sent») does not need the identity rebuild to be answerable. It also converts the
  standing audit assignment from a one-off reconstruction into 21 weeks of real handoff data.

### Architecture
1. **v1's writes become awaited and retried on the carried tables.** The parallel run rests on
   "v2 reads the same database", but `fire()` dispatches and forgets: a failed write logs a line,
   and a disconnected pool returns without writing at all. v2 would have spent 22 weeks reading a
   lagging, lossy projection. The await is **bounded** — timeout, backoff, then a counter — so a
   slow database can never stall a live WhatsApp reply.
2. **Postgres logical replication with real-time diffing** replaces the single cutover
   reconciliation. Drift found in week 4 is a bug; the same drift found in week 24 is a migration
   nobody can trust. **Slot lag is monitored and the slot is dropped at cutover as an explicit
   step**, because an orphaned replication slot fills the disk the product runs on.
3. **One host, path routing, shared session.** v2 sits behind the same hostname; screens move
   between systems without anyone learning two URLs. **The proxy never sits in front of the Gupshup
   webhook** — that path routes straight to v1 — so the number is never behind new infrastructure.
   Phase 0 also gains the second Fly app, its secret copies, its health endpoint and its smoke test,
   none of which the approved plan named.

### Code quality
4/9. **The four pure business-tier files become one shared package**, imported and run in process by
   both applications. Findings 4 and 8 were answered in ways that contradicted each other (HTTP for
   one implementation; local computation for speed); the package is the shape that delivers both
   properties, and it outlives v1 untouched. One definition, one test suite, one coverage gate.
5. **timestamptz in v2**, with the BIGINT conversion written once in the compatibility views v2
   already reads through. The reporting this plan exists to deliver is Riyadh-week date arithmetic,
   which is what the type is for. After cutover no conversion remains.
6. **A generated `openapi.yaml`, enforced by a build gate**, and one error shape (RFC 9457
   `problem+json`). Today the engine returns `{status}`, `{error}` and `{ok,error}` across 46, 38
   and 27 sites. Splitting into an API and a front end turns that into a contract between two
   codebases. Closes standards deviation **D-5**.

### Tests
7. **Testcontainers, seeded production-shaped fixtures, and 8 end-to-end flows.** Seven
   correctness-critical paths are all database behaviour and none is reachable by today's gate,
   which covers four pure modules and explicitly excludes Postgres: merge and its reversal, phone
   routing across a merge, the claim collision after many-to-one, the tap transaction, the view
   conversion, backfill idempotency, and the newly awaited write on the live WhatsApp path. Closes
   standards deviation **D-4**, which named Testcontainers as its own exit condition.

### Performance
10. **Reported outcome fields are promoted from JSONB to real columns.** The learning loop
    aggregates across them and this database has no JSON index of any kind, so there is no precedent
    to copy. Core reportable fields (outcome, objection category, competitor, budget signal, next
    step) become indexed columns shared by every product; `form` JSONB keeps only product-specific
    extras. A btree on `(account_id, occurred_at)` carries the account timeline.

### From the outside voice (Codex), verified in source
11. **An engagement's product is decided at write time, on per-product threads.** One WhatsApp
    conversation genuinely spans several products (`interest_tags` is one row per product per
    phone), and `productlock.ts:52` answers "what is this chat about **now**", which changes with
    every new message. Attributing history with it would show a product manager another product's
    conversation as evidence about theirs — silently, and looking correct. Threads are split per
    product with a human re-attribution tool; nothing is ever guessed.
12. **Merges are provisional for a reversal window before they become permanent.** The approved rule
    said post-merge rows stay on the target, which is tidy and wrong: a call logged against a merged
    identity has no recorded answer to "which clinic did the rep actually speak to". A window means
    a mistaken merge is caught before history accrues against it.
13. **A written charter, per-rep visibility defaults, and read auditing.** Every metric here is
    measured on one salesperson by one founder. Adoption is never an individual performance measure;
    there is a named owner and a dispute path; metrics require a customer response to move, so they
    cannot be satisfied by tapping alone; a quarterly sample is checked against real call records.
    A dashboard the measured people do not trust produces theatre, and theatre is what the learning
    loop would then be built on. **This review missed it entirely; Codex found it.**

**Recorded and declined.** Codex proposed cutting to a 12-week vertical slice (thin pilot, handoffs
on today's `entities`, sales inbox, tap-only outcomes, PM dashboard, pilot) deferring account
migration, dedup, configurable pipelines, voice, AI payback, CDC and cutover. The founder declined a
scope reduction twice — in office hours and again at this review's Step 0 — so it is recorded, not
re-argued. It remains the fallback if the handoff audit says the blindness is cheap.
